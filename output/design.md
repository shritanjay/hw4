# Design changes (Problem 10)

Goal: look like Yale Bulldog Blue, stay dark, and make it quicker to get from "browsing" to "this one, in my size."

| # | What changed | Why it helps shoppers stick around and buy |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bulldog Blue look, dark.** A Yale Blue (#00356B) announcement strip ("Officially licensed · 57 Broadway"), a serif "Yale Bulldog Blue *by Campus Customs*" wordmark, a Yale-blue banner hero with a large faint "Y", navy-black background, and Yale-blue buttons and active states. | Shoppers who know the real store recognize it immediately, so it feels official, not like a knock-off. "Officially licensed" and the street address up front build trust before anyone spends $58 on a crewneck. |
| 2 | **Yale-style serif type.** Headings, nav, prices, buttons and the wordmark use **EB Garamond**, an old-style serif in the same family as Yale's typeface. Body text stays a clean sans-serif for readability. | It reads "Yale" at a glance and makes the shop feel collegiate and premium, which supports full-price purchases. *Note:* Yale's own typeface is licensed for Yale use only and can't go in a public repo, so EB Garamond is the closest free match, bundled with the site (no Google Fonts call). |
| 3 | **Yale "thinking" indicator in chat.** The plain "…" is replaced by a serif **Yale** tile on Yale blue that pulses with a light sweep, next to "*Checking the shelves…*". The chat header and button use the same tile and font. Built in HTML/CSS (no logo image), and it stops animating for users who turn off motion. | Shoppers can see the assistant is working, and that it's checking real stock, so they wait for the answer instead of leaving. It also keeps the brand present in the chat. |
| 4 | **Tees first.** The catalogue, the "Featured: tees first" sort, the category chips, and the Home "Shop by style" tiles now go Tees ($32) → Long sleeves → Crewnecks → Hoodies → Quarter-zips → Jackets ($98). Home tiles show the starting price ("from $32"), and the main button is "Shop tees from $32". | The first thing people see is the cheapest way in. A $32 tee is an easy first purchase for students and visitors, and lower first prices keep more people browsing instead of bouncing. |
| 5 | **Departments.** Every product is grouped into **Classic Yale (22), Sports & Game Day (36), Residential Colleges (19), Grad & Professional Schools (13, including School of Management, Law, Medicine, Nursing…), Family (12)**. Products has a department bar with counts under "All departments", plus a one-line description of each. Home has "Shop by department" tiles, and the nav has Classic Yale / Sports / Family links. The shop assistant can filter by department too. | 102 items is a lot to scroll. People shop by *who it's for* (an SOM student, a Yale mom, a Davenport alum), so one click gets them to a short, relevant list where it's easier to find something and buy. |

**How it was checked:** EB Garamond is loaded and used for headings; the default order starts $32 → $45 → $58; the Grad & Professional Schools department shows 13 items with the SOM tee first; department counts add up to 102; exactly one nav link is highlighted per page (fixed a bug where all the department links looked active); and the chat shows the Yale tile + "Checking the shelves…" while answering. Asked "anything for a School of Management student?", the assistant filtered to that department and put the grid on the page.

Grouping is rule-based on product names (whole words, so "Brooks Brothers" isn't filed under Family). It lives in `tools.py` (`DEPARTMENTS`, `CATEGORY_ORDER`).
